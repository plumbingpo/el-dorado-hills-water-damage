Where Water Damage Starts in San Diego Properties

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for San Diego households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in San Diego. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many San Diego homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: A long, heavy rain can overwhelm street drains and send water across the yard and into the house. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.

The First Hour After Water Damage in San Diego

The hour between finding water and the crew arriving is when a homeowner in San Diego can do the most good. Safety comes before saving anything. Keep out of water that touches sockets, appliances or wiring, and only cut the power if the breaker box is in a dry place you can get to safely. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Then shut the water off. Close the valve at the fixture if there is one, or the main shutoff for the house. Then move what you can carry: rugs, electronics, papers, and small furniture go to a dry room. Put foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture so the wood does not stain the carpet.

Take photos and video of every affected room before you clean up, because your insurer will want to see the damage as it was. Mop or towel up small amounts of clean water if it is safe, and leave the rest to the crew heading to San Diego.
